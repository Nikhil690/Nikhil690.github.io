About

Exploring kernels, networks, and attack surfaces through systems programming.

I’m currently an undergraduate computer science student with a deep interest in low-level systems and kernel engineering. While coursework provides the theoretical backbone, most of my real learning happens during long debugging sessions — tracing misbehaving packets, instrumenting code paths, and reverse-engineering subsystems that refuse to behave.

I actively experiment with Go, C, and Rust across networking and systems projects, and I document every non-obvious insight to make the learning path reproducible for my peers (and my future self). I pick up new stacks quickly, deploy unfamiliar systems with confidence, and enjoy reducing complex runtimes into something observable, debuggable, and deterministic.

My current focus lies at the intersection of systems engineering and cybersecurity — exploring kernel internals, attack surfaces, and observability through eBPF, while building a stronger foundation in OS, networking, and secure system design.
